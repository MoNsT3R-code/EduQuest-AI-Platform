How the AI works

After every quest, a small model estimates how engaged a student is (Bronze, Silver or Gold) and the next quest adapts. Here is exactly what it uses, how well it works and what it can't do.

1. What the app measures

Nothing is typed in by the student. The app records these six things while they play:

InputHow it's measured
Quiz scoreScore in the quest just finished. Right first time counts fully, right on the second try counts half.
Learning timeTime spent answering in finished quests over the last 30 days (up to 10 hours). Idle time over 5 minutes on one question isn't counted.
Quests in this subjectQuests finished in this subject in the last 30 days (1 to 5).
Hints usedHints taken in the quest just finished.
Day streakDays in a row, up to today, with at least one finished quest (up to 30).
Quests finishedShare of quests started in the last 30 days that were finished.

4. How questions adapt

Each student has a rating per subject that rises when they answer well and falls when they miss, a little like a chess rating. The next question is the difficulty they're likely to get right about {{ (targets.Launchpad * 100) | int }}% of the time in Launchpad mode (Bronze), {{ (targets.Steady * 100) | int }}% in Steady mode (Silver) and {{ (targets.Challenge * 100) | int }}% in Challenge mode (Gold). Launchpad also hides the timer and adds a 50:50 hint.

5. Limits, and what we store

  • The training data is synthetic. The 200 records were generated for a course project, so the model has never seen real classrooms. Treat a tier as a nudge for the next quest, never as a grade.
  • Near a boundary it's unsure. When the model and the formula disagree, or it's under 60% confident, the student is told so.
  • Grade isn't an input. Questions are pitched at the student's grade, but the tier is never based on it.
  • Data kept: first name, grade, a hash of the picture password, quest answers and results. No IP addresses, emails or birthdays. Students can download or delete everything from My progress.
